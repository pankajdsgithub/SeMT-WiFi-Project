# Final Detailed Project Report (DPR)
## Campus Wi‑Fi Implementation in State Public Universities under Higher Education Department, Government of Odisha

## 1. Executive Summary
This Detailed Project Report (DPR) outlines the proposed design, implementation, governance and procurement structure for a campus Wi‑Fi deployment in selected state public universities under the Higher Education Department of Government of Odisha. The project is intended to strengthen the digital learning environment, improve access to academic resources, support administrative operations and provide a secure, centrally managed wireless ecosystem across university campuses.

The required solution is not limited to internet connectivity alone. It includes the creation of reliable campus wireless infrastructure, secure user access, central management, robust network operations, and institutional continuity across academic and administrative zones.

This DPR is prepared in a style aligned to government procurement practice, mirroring the structure used in the OCAC RFP for campus Wi‑Fi implementation in state public universities.

## 2. Project Context
Public universities in Odisha require modern digital infrastructure to support:
- classroom learning and blended education,
- academic research and digital access to resources,
- administrative productivity,
- student services and digital governance,
- secure campus mobility and visitor access,
- centralized campus monitoring and support.

Wireless access is now a foundational requirement for digital education and campus operations. It should be designed to support students, faculty, staff, classrooms, libraries, administrative blocks and public-facing or visitor access points.

## 3. Project Objectives
The project aims to achieve the following strategic and functional outcomes:
- provide high-quality wireless access across campus buildings and open areas,
- support academic and administrative digital activities,
- improve connectivity for students and staff,
- create a secure and centrally managed network architecture,
- reduce dependence on ad hoc or unmanaged access points,
- establish a scalable Wi‑Fi environment for future expansion,
- ensure reliable support and accountability through formal SLAs.

## 4. Project Sites
The reference tender structure covers the following universities:
- Utkal University, Bhubaneswar,
- Gangadhar Meher University, Sambalpur,
- Shree Jagannath Sanskrit Viswavidyalaya, Puri.

This report is designed as a model framework for similar SeMT Wi‑Fi projects and can be adapted to other campuses based on site conditions and stakeholder requirements.

## 5. Scope of the Project
The proposed Wi‑Fi project includes:
- survey and assessment of existing campus network availability,
- finalization of coverage zones and AP locations,
- design of network architecture and security segmentation,
- supply of access points, switches, controllers, licences and accessories,
- installation, integration and configuration,
- testing and commissioning,
- documentation, training and knowledge transfer,
- preventive maintenance and post-implementation support.

## 6. Functional Requirements
The solution shall support the following campus functions:
- learning in classrooms and seminar halls,
- student and faculty mobility across academic blocks,
- administration and office connectivity,
- library and digital reading environment,
- secure guest network for visitors,
- central monitoring and support for the university IT team,
- operations and maintenance through a formal support model.

## 7. Technical Design Approach
The technical design shall be based on a layered architecture:

### 7.1 Access Layer
- indoor and outdoor access points,
- high-density classroom support,
- coverage planning for libraries, corridors, admin blocks and open zones.

### 7.2 Distribution Layer
- managed PoE-capable switches,
- cable termination and redundancy,
- uplink aggregation to core or edge distribution points.

### 7.3 Core Layer
- central switching and routing,
- internet edge integration,
- segmentation between academic, administrative and guest traffic.

### 7.4 Management and Monitoring Layer
- centralized wireless management,
- client analytics, health monitoring and logs,
- policy-based management for authentication and access control.

## 8. Security Architecture
The Wi‑Fi solution must follow secure campus standards:
- separate SSIDs for staff, students, guest, and administrative access,
- encryption and authentication controls,
- policy enforcement and device control,
- secure management access,
- alerts and logging for suspicious or unauthorized access,
- protection against unmanaged external access.

## 9. Site Survey and Coverage Planning
The final design shall be based on a formal field survey, including:
- building layout analysis,
- AP placement optimization,
- building materials and RF interference study,
- user density and traffic load,
- power and network availability,
- outdoor and perimeter coverage requirements,
- future expansion considerations.

## 10. Proposed Technical Standards
The solution should adopt enterprise class standards such as:
- Wi‑Fi 6 or equivalent modern wireless technology,
- PoE-based access point operation,
- high-capacity managed switching,
- centralized wireless management,
- secure authentication and segmentation,
- support for roaming and seamless connectivity.

## 11. Equipment and Services Scope
The selected system integrator shall provide:
- APs and access layer equipment,
- managed switching equipment,
- controller/licensing and management software,
- installation accessories and mounting hardware,
- cable and structured network infrastructure,
- project engineering and testing,
- documentation and training,
- warranty and support services.

## 12. Implementation Methodology
The implementation should be carried out in clearly defined stages:

### Stage 1: Mobilisation and Survey
- project kickoff,
- detailed site walkthrough,
- assessment of existing infrastructure,
- coverage mapping.

### Stage 2: Design Finalisation
- final AP placement plan,
- network and security design,
- review and approval by stakeholders.

### Stage 3: Supply and Installation
- equipment delivery,
- cabling, mounting and installation,
- configuration and integration,
- validation of logical and physical connectivity.

### Stage 4: Testing and Acceptance
- RF validation,
- authentication tests,
- guest access tests,
- performance evaluation,
- acceptance certificate issuance.

### Stage 5: Handover and Support
- training,
- documentation handover,
- warranty and support activation,
- monitoring readiness.

## 13. Project Governance
The project requires formal governance involving:
- department/sponsor,
- university nodal officers,
- OCAC technical support / authority,
- system integrator project leader,
- IT and network teams of the university,
- acceptance panel for commissioning.

## 14. Risk Management
The project should anticipate and mitigate risks such as:
- poor AP placement,
- uneven RF coverage,
- power and network bottlenecks,
- interoperability issues,
- security and access policy gaps,
- delay in site readiness,
- poor user adoption after handover.

## 15. Bid Evaluation and Procurement Approach
The tender shall use a structured procurement model with:
- pre-qualification screening,
- technical evaluation,
- commercial evaluation,
- final award based on compliance and technical suitability.

This ensures that the selected vendor is technically capable, service-oriented and financially realistic.

## 16. Payment Schedule Summary
The procurement process should align payment milestones to project progress and acceptance. A typical model includes:
- mobilisation and survey,
- supply of equipment,
- installation and integration,
- testing and commissioning,
- training and handover,
- warranty and support activation.

## 17. Warranty and SLA Requirements
The winning bidder shall provide:
- equipment warranty as per OEM standards,
- SLA support for network uptime,
- preventive maintenance,
- competence of support engineers,
- issue resolution in defined response and resolution windows,
- spare support commitments.

## 18. Operational Sustainability
The long-term success of the campus Wi‑Fi project depends on:
- central management capability,
- user support processes,
- continuous monitoring,
- periodic capacity review,
- firmware and security updates,
- alignment with institutional digital strategy.

## 19. Conclusion
The campus Wi‑Fi project is a strategic digital infrastructure initiative for state public universities. It must be implemented as a secure, scalable and governable digital campus service rather than a simple access-point installation. A procurement model built on technical accountability, design quality, performance testing and service support is therefore essential.

This DPR is provided as a ready-to-adapt government project documentation template for SeMT Wi‑Fi implementation and should be refined with final site survey data, university-specific campus conditions and formal authority approvals before tender issuance.
